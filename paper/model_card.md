# Model card — Longitudinal Health Foundation Model (LHFM) v0.1

## Overview

LHFM is a self-supervised multimodal transformer over per-day wearable,
smartphone, and environmental signals. Pretrained with masked-feature
reconstruction, next-day state prediction, and a participant-trajectory
contrastive objective. Fine-tuned with four binary risk heads.

## Intended use

- **Primary**: methodological research on representation learning for
  longitudinal passive-sensing data. Specifically, work investigating
  multimodal fusion, missingness-aware modelling, personal-baseline
  modelling, and climate-health integration.
- **Secondary**: a teaching scaffold for graduate courses on digital health
  and computational psychiatry.
- **Out of scope**: any clinical decision-making, diagnosis, treatment
  selection, triage, prognosis estimation, insurance underwriting,
  employment screening, or law-enforcement use.

## Inputs

- A 14-day window of per-day records covering wearable, smartphone, EMA,
  and environmental signals. Any field may be missing.
- A slowly varying participant profile (age, sex, chronotype, baseline
  sleep need, baseline HRV).

## Outputs

- Four probability estimates between 0 and 1 (low mood, high stress, sleep
  disruption, climate vulnerability).
- A short, rule-based human-readable explanation.
- A confidence score (distance from the 0.5 decision boundary).
- An explicit disclaimer marking the model as non-clinical.

## Training data

Synthetic only. 250 simulated participants × 90 days, generated by
`src/data/synthetic_generator.py`. No real human-subject data is used. See
`paper/data_card.md` for details.

## Performance

Reported on the held-out synthetic test split in
`results/tables/metrics_test.csv`. AUROC values should be interpreted as
**evidence that the architecture and pipeline are functioning end-to-end**,
not as estimates of how it would perform on real data.

## Limitations

- Trained on synthetic data; effect sizes are not clinically calibrated.
- Downstream targets are proxies derived from coarse EMA scales, not
  validated clinical instruments.
- No external validation cohort. No real-world deployment data.
- The participant embedding is keyed by integer ID; transferring the model
  to a new cohort requires retraining or a different personalization
  strategy.
- Calibration has only been measured on the synthetic test split.
- The "explanation" is a hand-written rule set, not a faithful
  interpretation of the underlying neural network.

## Risks

Even though the model is non-clinical, a few categories of harm warrant
explicit naming:

- **Surveillance creep.** Passive sensing is the kind of capability that
  invites mission-creep by employers, schools, insurers, and the carceral
  system. Anyone extending LHFM should treat consent, deletion rights, and
  purpose-limitation as first-class design constraints.
- **Algorithmic bias.** No fairness audits have been performed; the
  synthetic cohort cannot reveal real-world disparities.
- **Anchoring effects on clinicians.** Even probabilistic outputs from a
  research model can anchor downstream human judgement. Outputs should not
  be presented to anyone making care decisions.
- **Over-attribution of mood to environment.** Climate features can give
  the misleading impression that mood is largely environmentally caused.

## Recommendations

- Do not deploy in any patient-facing setting.
- For research collaboration, run all model outputs through a separate
  human-subjects review.
- Treat the synthetic data and its embedded causal assumptions as a
  *starting point for hypothesis generation*, not as ground truth.
